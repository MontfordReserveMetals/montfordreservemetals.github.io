## Purpose

This document expands the James Montford visual system beyond portraits and profile photography. These prompts are built for gold and silver bullion scenes, vault imagery, desk spreads, cigars, whiskey, and other high-value environments that reinforce the Montford Reserve Metals mood without drifting into cheap excess.

## Tone Target

The tone should be:

- wealthy but restrained
- tactile and atmospheric
- old-money masculine
- private-office luxury, not nightclub luxury
- high-value metals handled with familiarity

## Scene Guardrails

- Keep the setting elegant and composed.
- Bars, coins, cigars, and whiskey should feel curated and expensive, not chaotic.
- James should still read as a disciplined buyer, not a playboy caricature.
- Lean toward walnut, brass, leather, crystal, and warm low light.
- Avoid casino, gangster, or music-video energy.

## Global Constraints

- no celebrity likeness
- no text
- no watermark
- no tacky excess
- no neon, nightclub, or casino lighting
- no piles of random cash
- no exaggerated wealth clichés
- no sloppy drinking or messy smoke clouds
- no cartoonish pirate-treasure presentation of bullion

## Iteration Notes

If these scenes start drifting too far into stereotype, correct in this order:

- reduce smoke
- reduce object count
- clean up the desk composition
- soften the whiskey emphasis
- make the bullion more organized and believable
- make James more composed and less performative

## Approval Questions

- Do you want cigars and whiskey to appear often or only in selected premium scenes?
- Should vault scenes feel more classic old-world or more modern institutional luxury?
- Should bullion be the main visual hero in these scenes, or should James remain the main focal point?
- Do you want some of these scenes to feel more collector-oriented and some more private-client oriented?
